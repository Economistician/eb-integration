SYSTEM: Electric Barometer (EB)

PURPOSE
Electric Barometer is a forecasting readiness and governance framework for
panel-based time-series forecasting in real-world operational environments.
Its goal is not accuracy optimization alone, but controlled, auditable,
cost-aware forecast usage under explicit governance.

This file defines the canonical system worldview, pipeline ordering, and
non-negotiable invariants for the Electric Barometer ecosystem.

---

SYSTEM PRINCIPLES (NON-NEGOTIABLE)

1. Metrics are descriptive, not binding.
   Metrics (including FRS, NSL, UD, HR@τ, CWSL, and related variants) describe
   readiness, risk, and operational alignment. Metrics alone do not authorize,
   block, or permit forecast adjustment, fallback, or execution behavior.

2. Calibration defines evaluation context, not decisions.
   Calibration artifacts (e.g., cost ratios, tolerance grids, selected τ)
   define the evaluation envelope in which metrics are computed. Calibration
   does not alter forecasts, does not gate execution, and does not replace
   governance.

3. Governance is the single binding decision layer.
   No other component may authorize forecast adjustment, fallback, or execution
   behavior. Governance decisions may be computed as artifacts in downstream
   repositories, but their authority is system-level.

4. Structural admissibility precedes adjustment.
   Demand representation compatibility (DQC) and forecast primitive
   compatibility (FPC) must be evaluated before any adjustment or fallback
   logic is considered.

5. Adjustment is optional, bounded, and deterministic.
   The Readiness Adjustment Layer (RAL) applies only when explicitly permitted
   by Governance. Adjustments must be bounded, reproducible, and auditable.
   Identity (no-op) is always a valid outcome.

6. Optional strictness is allowed upstream, never downstream.
   Informative diagnostics (e.g., FAS) may influence modeling strategy or
   resource allocation but may not act as binding gates.

7. Contracts are canonical.
   Data, forecast, diagnostic, and policy artifacts must conform to versioned
   Electric Barometer contracts. Contracts are backward-compatible once
   published.

---

CANONICAL PIPELINE (AUTHORITATIVE ORDER)

0. Contractify Demand
   Raw demand is transformed into contract-valid panel artifacts defining
   identity, time, value, and observability.

1. Baseline Forecast
   A cheap, universal baseline forecast is produced for all slices.
   The baseline exists to expose error anatomy, not to maximize performance.

2. Forecast Admissibility Surface (FAS) — OPTIONAL
   FAS is applied to baseline forecasts to diagnose admissibility regimes,
   structural fragility, and modeling risk.
   - FAS is informative, not binding.
   - FAS may be enabled or disabled based on strictness.
   - Pipelines may be compared with and without FAS.

3. Model Zoo / Model Selection
   One or more forecasting models are trained and/or selected.
   Metrics may be used here for comparative model selection only.
   FAS outputs may inform modeling strategy or resource allocation, but do not
   authorize or block outcomes.

4. Evaluation Metrics (Readiness Diagnostics)
   Forecast artifacts are evaluated using the Electric Barometer metric suite.
   Metrics are diagnostic inputs only and include paired metric–calibration
   constructs:

   Cost / magnitude primitives:
   - CWSL (Cost-Weighted Shortfall Loss)
   - CWSLR (Cost-ratio sensitivity and calibration)

   Tolerance / acceptability primitives:
   - HR@τ (Hit Rate within Tolerance)
   - HR@τ sensitivity and calibration (tolerance grids, selection rules,
     safeguards)

   Additional readiness diagnostics:
   - NSL (No-Shortfall Level)
   - UD (Underbuild Depth)
   - FRS (Forecast Readiness Score)

   Calibration artifacts (e.g., cost ratios, tolerance grids, selected τ) define
   the evaluation context but do not gate or authorize decisions.

5. Demand Quantization Compatibility (DQC)
   DQC determines the admissible demand representation (e.g., raw vs quantized).
   DQC is structural and representation-only.

6. Forecast Primitive Compatibility (FPC)
   FPC evaluates whether the forecast primitive is compatible with adjustment
   within the DQC-declared representation.
   DQC MUST precede FPC.

7. Governance
   Governance composes:
   - evaluation diagnostics,
   - calibration context (cost ratios, tolerances),
   - DQC results,
   - FPC results,
   - explicit policy thresholds
   into a single binding decision artifact governing adjustment and fallback.

8. Readiness Adjustment Layer (RAL) — IF PERMITTED
   RAL applies bounded, deterministic adjustments only when permitted by
   Governance.

9. Serving / Execution
   The final forecast artifact (adjusted, baseline, or fallback) is served with
   full governance and adjustment traceability.

---

REPOSITORY ROLES (SYSTEM VIEW)

- eb-contracts:
  Defines canonical schemas, invariants, and validation rules.

- eb-metrics:
  Implements metric primitives and calibration utilities.
  No policy, gating, or execution logic.

- eb-evaluation:
  Computes diagnostics, metrics, and governance artifacts.
  No optimization or execution authority.

- eb-optimization:
  Tunes parameters and decision policies using historical data.
  Produces reproducible artifacts; does not execute decisions.

- eb-adapters:
  Bridges external models and tools into EB contracts.

- electric-barometer:
  Orchestrates forecasting workflows and model selection.

- eb-integration:
  System-level CI/CD and cross-package integration validation.
  Ensures ecosystem compatibility; contains no business logic.

- eb-docs / eb-papers:
  Human-facing documentation and formal references.
  These must not contradict this file.

---

DISALLOWED SYSTEM BEHAVIOR

- Using metrics (including FRS or HR@τ) as hard gates
- Performing adjustment or fallback without Governance permission
- Evaluating FPC before DQC
- Treating FAS as a binding authorization
- Mutating forecasts during evaluation
- Using calibration to favor specific models ex post
- Hiding fallback or adjustment behavior outside governance
